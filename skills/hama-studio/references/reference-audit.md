# Reference detail-page audit

Use this procedure when the user gives a reference URL and asks for a new detail page in its style or quality. The reference is a model for selling logic and presentation, not evidence about the user's product.

## Capture the complete page

1. Record the exact URL, access date, and page type. Separate platform UI (gallery, price, purchase controls, navigation) from the seller-authored detail story. Inspect the authored story from its first block through its final information and FAQ blocks; expand collapsed content and revisit the bottom after lazy loading.
2. Build an ordered ledger. Give each section and each significant still, GIF, animated WebP, or video an ID and source location. Record the buyer question, claim or benefit, visual treatment, proof, transition to the next section, and screen length or relative pacing. Count all sections and media so that a long page is not reduced to its first few screens.
3. For each media item, record the rendered role, actual format, dimensions and duration when available, and whether it moves. Do not infer animation from its extension or from one screenshot. Check lazy-loading attributes or the rendered media URL when a placeholder hides the real asset.
4. Inspect every animation over time. Capture or observe its opening, meaningful action changes, midpoint, ending, and loop seam. For embedded video, inspect the opening, major scene changes, and ending. Note the exact action, on-screen copy, camera treatment, and why movement helps sell the product. Sample more moments when a clip contains several scenes. If a tool cannot play or sample the asset, mark it unverified instead of treating it as a still.
5. Respect access controls. If the page or media is blocked, record the visible portion and the specific gap. Ask the user for the smallest useful authorized handoff, such as a screen recording, GIF file, or page export. Do not claim a full-reference audit from a text scrape or incomplete screenshot set.

## Explain the selling system

Map the page's actual sequence: hook, problem, promise, demonstrations, use cases, credibility, objections, options, purchase details, and close, where present. Identify how the page expands a small number of product benefits without simply repeating slogans: different buyer questions, contexts of use, close-ups, comparisons with valid evidence, demonstrations, and changes in visual scale or tempo. Distinguish observed content from an inference about why it works.

Record its design grammar: palette and contrast, typography hierarchy, image-to-copy balance, section density, whitespace, composition changes, and placement of motion. Identify patterns worth transferring and elements that belong to the reference's specific brand or product. Never transfer its photos, illustrations, wording, reviews, certifications, measurements, or performance claims to the user's product.

## Handoff to production

Before storyboarding, produce five linked items: (1) full ordered section map, (2) complete media and motion register, (3) persuasion arc and pacing analysis, (4) design grammar, and (5) transferable principles tied to the user's product evidence. For each proposed new section, state which source photo, fact, demonstration, or additional evidence can support it. If a reference technique would require an unsupported product claim or unverified action, replace that technique with a truthful one.

Check the first, middle, and final portions independently. Reconcile section and media counts against the original page. A complete audit accounts for every important block and every animated item or explicitly marks it inaccessible. If a substantial part remains unknown, report partial coverage and keep the missing-reference risk visible during production.
