# Detail-page quality gate — 100 points

Score the **rendered, full-length page and actual encoded motion**, not a plan. Cite concrete screens or files for every score. This rubric intentionally gives visual persuasion and temporal demonstration more weight than length.

| Criterion | Points | Full-credit evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Buyer persuasion and benefit expansion | 25 | The first screens create immediate interest; every major benefit progresses through a recognizable doubt, action/mechanism, payoff, and proof or honest limitation. Long sections answer new questions rather than repeat adjectives. |
| Visual hierarchy and mobile readability | 20 | One takeaway per screen; decisive Korean headline readable at phone size; supporting copy short and subordinate; product/action dominates; no run of paragraph-led screens. |
| Art direction and color rhythm | 15 | Restrained palette derived from the product, clear role for each color, coherent adjacent-screen transitions, strong image consistency, and intentional quiet/impact rhythm. |
| Product and claim fidelity | 15 | The exact option, geometry, parts, color, dimensions, reviews, brand facts, and disclosure entries match sources. Unverified numbers, comparative claims, customer praise, and staged demonstrations do not masquerade as proof. |
| Motion as explanation | 15 | Every important temporal feature that is claimed is shown with a complete, understandable sequence. At least one actual encoded use demonstration when the product's use is central to the pitch; start/action/end, hand contact, final physical state, loop, mobile legibility, and fallback still checked. A source-backed AI recreation can explain ordinary use but cannot prove measured performance. Decorative motion alone earns at most 3/15. |
| HTML preview and delivery | 10 | One continuous rendered HTML page with working inline motion and a still fallback, editable source, normally 20 ordered exports, one combined final product-information/size panel, full preview, claim/media provenance, and no visual defects. An optional standalone HTML bundle opens without a local server. |

**Pass:** at least 90/100, and at least 20/25 persuasion, 16/20 readability, 12/15 art direction, 12/15 fidelity, and 12/15 motion when the product has a temporal use or mechanism. A high total cannot cancel a failed minimum.

## Hard gates

1. **Two-second screen test:** In phone-width preview, a reviewer can name the main benefit or buyer question on each persuasive screen without reading a paragraph. If multiple consecutive screens are text-led, redesign them.
2. **Type test:** Read every export at its intended mobile display width. Main Korean claims must be immediately legible; size alone is insufficient if contrast, line breaks, or visual noise harms reading. Inspect the smallest buyer-relevant text separately.
3. **Color sequence test:** Review the full-page thumbnail and 3–5 adjacent screens. Each background and accent color has a documented role; mismatched or arbitrary shifts fail even if individual screens look polished.
4. **Persuasion test:** The storyboard and rendered page both show why a buyer should care, how the feature works, what changes in real use, and what evidence/limitation supports it. A product beauty shot plus labels does not pass.
5. **Motion truth test:** For a product whose use depends on opening, rotating, fitting, etc., render and inspect a full action at start, contact point, middle, and end. A pan/zoom over a still or a generated clip that approaches a ring without visibly completing attachment/detachment fails. When source photos establish the parts and open/closed states, an accurately rendered use recreation can satisfy the *explanation* gate. Record its provenance internally; do not add a routine on-screen AI badge. It cannot validate load, durability, or other performance. If geometry drifts or completion is unclear, mark this gate failed and explain the needed footage or better recreation.
6. **Source and option test:** Copy, dimensions, comparisons, certificates, tests, and generated product geometry must match the selected option and trace to evidence. Unknowns remain unknown. Mark pictured props that are not part of the sale so staged key rings or bags do not look included.
7. **Reference transfer test:** Inspect the reference through its ending and its animated media. The new page visibly carries over useful storytelling and pacing principles without copying protected content.
8. **HTML delivery test:** Open the actual continuous HTML page in Codex or a browser. Check top, middle, and ending in sequence; verify inline GIF, animated WebP, or video playback and a still fallback. Separate PNG exports alone do not pass. If local-file opening is blocked by browser security policy, do not circumvent it; verify the editable HTML locally where already authorized, check the standalone bundle's media integrity, supply the one-file HTML and full-page static preview, and record that the bundled file was not opened in the app. Record any Coupang upload field as `untested` until the user wants to prepare publication and the exact seller editor has been checked.
9. **Requested WebP allocation and timing test:** Record the user's intake target and the finished page's primary visual panel total. Count only distinct, reviewed, actually animated WebPs in primary panels. Each default clip is 3 seconds, 20 fps, 60 frames; use 4 seconds, 20 fps, 80 frames when the action needs longer. Verify the encoded frame count, 50 ms timing, smooth action, and loop seam. Duplicating a handful of stills to reach 60/80, interpolated ghosting, a static WebP, or a renamed GIF fails. If the target cannot be met without false product motion or poor quality, explain the exact shortfall; do not mark the page complete or silently lower the target.
10. **Page structure test:** Produce 20 numbered, exportable panels by default. Panels 1–19 answer distinct buyer questions; do not pad with duplicated text or near-identical images. If the user requested a different total, use that total. Reserve only the final panel for combined product information and size.
11. **Final information/size test:** Before production, confirm brand, final product name, material, every sold color/option, and what each measured size means. The final panel has those five buyer-facing fields and option images/verified measurements together, with units. S/M/L or color variants are not merged into one invented specification. Missing or ambiguous values stop buyer-facing production until clarified.
12. **Separate listing-disclosure test:** Keep the five-field designed panel separate from the currently applicable category disclosure list. Verify origin, age guidance, warnings, manufacturer/importer, and other category-required information in the seller listing workflow. Do not invent or silently omit mandatory fields; unresolved fields block a publication-ready label even when the designed preview is complete.
13. **Review/brand truth test:** Genuine product reviews may appear in the middle with their source; fabricated customers, ratings, purchases, and praise never appear as reviews. When reviews are absent, use a labeled scenario or FAQ. Use the brand confirmed for the selected SKU consistently, without invented history or unsupported claims.
14. **Supplier-image and label test:** Compare the buyer-facing HTML, ordered exports, animated frames, and standalone bundle against the source manifest. No 1688/supplier source pixels or source files may appear, even as a crop or recolor. Generated studio, detail, and use shots must be new and SKU-faithful. Remove routine AI-generation badges from buyer-facing panels while retaining provenance in the internal manifest and checking any legally required disclosure separately.

When a gate fails, record the affected screen, the buyer-facing consequence, the specific corrective action, and the evidence needed to recheck. Fix the highest-impact failure, rerender, and score again. Do not lower this rubric to fit a draft.
